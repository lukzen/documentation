Business Plan & Interactive Revenue Calculator

1. Executive Summary

Ergos Continental operates a multi-GDS hotel aggregation platform serving travel agencies across Europe and Latin America, with global hotel inventory through Juniper (~9,000 hotels), Hoteltec, Dingus, and soon Restel/Hotelbeds (200K+ hotels). The platform is pre-launch with zero active agencies.

The integration of Mozio's ground transportation API (~2 months out) represents the highest-leverage ancillary revenue opportunity: it monetizes an existing transaction flow (hotel bookings) with zero incremental customer acquisition cost, adds a service that agencies currently fulfill manually or not at all, and creates a bundling dynamic that increases platform stickiness.

Two Sales Channels: Ergos will sell hotel + transfer packages through (A) the B2B agency platform (agencies resell to their clients) and (B) a B2C direct webapp (Ergos sells directly to end consumers at its own retail prices, keeping the full margin).

4. B2C Direct Channel — Ergos Consumer Webapp

The opportunity: Ergos already has the hotel inventory and (soon) transfer supply. A consumer-facing webapp lets Ergos sell directly to travelers at retail prices, keeping the full margin that would otherwise be split with agencies. The B2C channel runs in parallel to B2B — they don't cannibalize each other because agencies serve different customer segments (corporate, groups, offline travelers).

5. Hotelbeds Competitive Tension

The elephant in the room: Agencies accessing Hotelbeds hotels via Ergos/Restel may also access "Transfers by Hotelbeds" directly.

Agency Segment% of ErgosThreatStrategy
WITH direct Hotelbeds account ~40-50% HIGH Win on UX integration: one workflow, one invoice, one confirmation. Don't compete on price.
WITHOUT direct Hotelbeds (use Ergos as access) ~30-40% LOW Sweet spot. Won't set up direct Hotelbeds just for transfers.
Juniper/Hoteltec/Dingus only ~20-30% NONE Natural fit. Ergos is the only integrated transfer option.

Strategic Implications

  1. Segment your GTM. Prioritize agencies WITHOUT direct Hotelbeds access for transfer launch.
  2. Win on integration, not price. One booking, one invoice, one support point.
  3. Consider hybrid supply. Long-term, integrate BOTH Mozio AND Hotelbeds transfers — become the transfer aggregator.
  4. Manage the Hotelbeds relationship. Lead with "complete your booking on one platform," not "better than Hotelbeds."
  5. Mozio covers routes Hotelbeds doesn't. Secondary cities, emerging markets — genuine differentiation.

Glossary — Key Terms Explained

Every metric and term used in this business plan and calculator, explained in plain language.

Rg Registered Agencies
Travel agencies that have signed up for an Ergos account. They may or may not be actively using the platform.
e.g., 300 agencies signed up in Year 1
Ac Active Agencies
Registered agencies that make at least one hotel booking per month. This is the "activation rate" applied to registered agencies.
40% activation = 120 active out of 300 registered
At Attachment Rate
The percentage of hotel bookings where a ground transfer is also booked. This is the single most important metric — a small change in attachment rate has a massive effect on revenue.
4% attachment on 55K hotel bookings = 2,200 transfers
Ch Churn Rate
The percentage of agencies that stop using Ergos each year. High churn means you need to constantly replace lost agencies with new ones.
25% churn = losing 75 of 300 agencies per year
CR Churn Reduction (Multi-Product)
How much churn decreases when agencies use both hotels AND transfers. Agencies using multiple products are "stickier" — harder to lose to competitors.
20% reduction on 25% base churn = effective churn drops to 20%
GBV Gross Booking Value
The total dollar value of all transfer bookings before any costs are subtracted. This is what the end customer pays.
2,200 transfers x avg $55 = $121K GBV
Co Commission Rate
The percentage of GBV that Mozio pays Ergos as revenue. This is Ergos's gross income before operational costs.
12% commission on $121K GBV = $14.5K gross revenue
Op Operational Cost per Transfer
The cost Ergos pays per transfer for payment processing (~$0.95), API infrastructure (~$0.15), and customer support (~$0.75). Total ~$1.85.
2,200 transfers x $1.85 = $4,070 total op costs
NR Net Revenue
What Ergos actually keeps after all costs. Calculated as: GBV x Commission Rate - Operational Costs. This is the real bottom-line number.
$14.5K gross - $4.1K ops = $10.4K net revenue
RT Round-Trip Share
What percentage of transfer bookings are round-trips (airport→hotel + hotel→airport) vs. one-way only. Round-trips have ~1.85x the revenue of one-way.
60% round-trip share means 6 out of 10 bookings include both legs
B2B Business-to-Business Channel
Ergos sells to travel agencies, who then resell to their end customers. Ergos earns a commission; the agency adds their own markup. Lower margin per booking but zero customer acquisition cost.
B2C Business-to-Consumer Channel
Ergos sells directly to end travelers via its own webapp. Ergos sets the full retail price and keeps the entire margin (no agency cut). Higher margin (~2x B2B) but requires marketing spend to acquire customers.
CAC Customer Acquisition Cost
How much Ergos spends on marketing (Google Ads, SEO, social) to acquire one B2C customer. Must be lower than the net margin per booking for the channel to be profitable.
$8 net margin per transfer - $5 CAC = $3 true profit
Se Sensitivity Analysis
Shows how much total revenue changes when you increase a single input by a fixed amount. Helps identify which variables matter most — focus your energy on the ones with the highest impact.
"+2pp" means increasing from 4% to 6% (percentage points)
GDS Global Distribution System
Technology platforms that connect hotel/travel suppliers to agencies and booking platforms. Ergos aggregates multiple GDS sources (Juniper, Restel, etc.) so agencies can compare rates across all of them in one place.
pp Percentage Points
The absolute difference between two percentages. "Increasing from 4% to 6%" is an increase of 2 percentage points (not 50%). Used in the sensitivity analysis to show clear, fixed-size changes.
