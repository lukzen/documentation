Where the card is captured — and why it matters

Recommended (SAQ-A): the card form is served and rendered by TropiPay on their own domain. Ergos only ever holds a payment reference and a checkout link. PCI scope is ~22 controls, and 3-D Secure / SCA is the provider’s problem.

Direct capture (SAQ-D): the identical-looking form lives in our DOM (payment-card-number, payment-cvv…) and posts the PAN to our backend. ~300 controls, annual audit, pen-testing. The card-entry screen in this prototype is that direct-capture form — it is drawn inside a red dashed frame whenever it is ours, and inside a teal “hosted” frame when it stands in for TropiPay’s page.

any other Luhn-valid PANTreated as approved.

Known discrepancy, stated deliberately: 4100 0000 0000 0001 does not satisfy the Luhn checksum (its correct check digit is 2). It is accepted here as an explicit documented trigger so the decline path stays reachable while the Luhn gate remains real. 4100 0000 0000 0002 is the Luhn-valid equivalent and triggers the same decline — prefer it in new Cypress specs.

Expiry: any future MM/YY. CVV: any 3 digits (4 for American Express). Cardholder name: any 2+ characters.

Checking our API… attempt 1

This browser — advisory

Polls GET /api/bookings/…/payment on our own API. It only reports state. Closing the tab, losing the network, or paying on another device changes nothing about the outcome.

TropiPay webhook — authoritative

TropiPay POSTs a signed notification to our backend. That is what moves the booking to CONFIRMED and sends the voucher — once, idempotently, even if the webhook is retried and even if the browser never returns.

Prototype note: no request is made. The timer below stands in for the poll loop so the waiting state, the timeout and the “webhook never arrived” state can all be reviewed. Force any of them from the control panel.
