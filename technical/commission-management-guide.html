1. The Mental Model Start here

The whole commission system is built on one idea: commission values cascade down 5 layers, and a more-specific layer can override a less-specific one.

When the engine prices a booking for a given combination of (hotel, agency, agent), it walks the layers in order. For each field, the most-specific value wins. The result is the effective commission used in the math.

1
Global Defaults
Platform-wide baseline that applies to every booking unless overridden below.
baseCommission · chainDiscountPct · earningPercentage · operationalExpense
2
Hotel (with chain inheritance)
Per-hotel overrides for Ergos markup and chain discount. baseCommission cascades hotel → linked chain → global default; chainDiscountPct cascades hotel → linked chain (vendor-gated) → 0.
baseCommission · chainDiscountPct · chainId (link to chain)
3
Agency Rebate (per hotel)
The rebate this agency gets from Ergos for bookings at this specific hotel.
rebatePct
4
Agent Tier · read-only
Tier defaults are policy constants in code (not editable from the UI). Each agent belongs to one tier: Starter / Growth / Elite / Strategic.
earningRange · default earningPct · default opsExpense
5
Agent-Hotel Override
A specific (agent, hotel) earning override that beats the tier default.
earningPctOverride
Why this design? Most bookings use the global or hotel-level defaults. Special cases (a strategic agency, a key agent's pet hotel) need surgical overrides without rewriting the whole policy. The cascade lets you set baseline policy once and tweak only the exceptions.
Storage convention — WHOLE PERCENT integers (0–100). Every commission / discount / rebate / markup field on the platform is stored, transported via API, and displayed as a whole-percent integer. 13 means 13%, 4 means 4%, 0 means none. The commission engine converts to decimal once internally via pct(n) = n/100. This includes the agency-side defaultMarkupPct on the TravelAgency model and its booking snapshot agencyMarkupPctSnapshot — even though those sit outside the commission engine's math, they follow the same integer convention for consistency across the codebase, the UI input controls, and the API contract.
Policy revision — 2026-05-27. Two material changes to baseCommission that affect everything below:
  • New default is 3% (was 15%). Stakeholders ratified a min 1% / average 3% / max 100% policy. The global default in CommissionDefaults dropped from 15 → 3 and a one-shot migration (migrate-base-commission-to-conservative.ts) reset existing hotels to 3 where they were below the new floor or above the threshold without a chain discount. The schema enforces min: 1, max: 100; the ≥5% + chain-discount relationship is informational guidance in the UI, not an enforced rule. (A short-lived enforcement variant existed briefly the same day and was rolled back per stakeholder feedback.)
  • Chain-level baseCommission cascade. The CommissionChain model now carries an optional baseCommission field. The Layer-2 resolution order is hotel.baseCommission → chain.baseCommission → global default. Useful for the Cuba chains (DOMINA, MELIA, IBEROSTAR, GRANCARIBE, GAVIOTA) where one chain-wide markup avoids per-property edits across dozens of hotels. The pure helper resolveBaseCommission(hotel, chain, globalDefault) mirrors the existing resolveHotelChainDiscount.

Which party owns each variable?

The 5 layers above are policy locations. The fields you edit inside those layers each represent a different party's money. The most common confusion is over earningPercentage — it is the sales agent's cut, not Ergos's and not the agency's.

Quick mental map:
  • Ergos earns via baseCommission (the markup that creates ergosGross).
  • The travel agency earns via rebatePct (cashback from Ergos) and via its own customer-facing markup (captured separately in the agency app).
  • The sales agent earns via earningPercentage — the agent's share of Ergos's gross margin, not of sell price.
Field Stored on Whose money? Meaning Unit / range
baseCommission Hotel · CommissionDefaults Ergos Markup applied on top of the GDS's adjusted cost. Drives ergosSell = adjustedCost × (1 + baseCommission/100). Higher → Ergos earns more. Default 3 per 2026-05-27 policy; chain or per-hotel overrides bump this for chain-discounted or rebate-paying bookings. 1–100 integer (3 = 3%)
chainDiscountPct Hotel · Chain · CommissionDefaults GDS (cost-side) Negotiated chain discount as a percentage off GDS net. 15 = 15% off cost. Higher → better margin for Ergos. Default 0 means no discount. Schema validators on the Hotel model enforce min: 0, max: 100. 0–100 integer (15 = 15% off)
rebatePct per (Agency, Hotel) Travel agency Cashback paid to the agency, taken off ergosSell. 4 = 4% rebate. In-house Ergos agency uses 0. Stored in the travel_agency_commission collection as commissionPercentage; surfaced via API/UI as rebatePct. 0–100 integer (4 = 4%)
earningPercentage SalesAgent · CommissionDefaults Sales agent Agent's slice of Ergos's GROSS margin — NOT of sell, NOT Ergos's net, NOT the agency's. 24 = the agent keeps 24% of ergosGross. Default per tier; per-(agent, hotel) overrides win. Per-agent-hotel overrides are stored in sales_agent_commission as commissionPercentage; surfaced via API/UI as earningPctOverride. 0–100 integer (no runtime cap; tier defaults max out at 32%)
operationalExpense SalesAgent · CommissionDefaults Agent's overhead Agent's operational expense modeled as a percentage of Ergos gross (back-office cost, dispute handling, ramp cost). Deducted alongside agent earning. 0–100 integer
Older API surfaces use the alias commissionPercentage on the TravelAgency model and on the legacy sales-agent endpoint. These are aliases of the same concepts; the canonical names used end-to-end by the commission engine and stored on every booking snapshot are the ones in the table above.

3. Screen: Commission Chains /commission-chains

This screen manages the chain registry. A chain represents a contractual relationship with a hotel chain (Meliá, Iberostar, etc.) — including any negotiated discount, contract status, and dates.

Why are chains separate from the policy editor? A chain is metadata about a contract (status, dates, notes) — not commission policy directly. The chain doesn't auto-cascade to hotels. Instead, admins create the chain once, link hotels, and then explicitly apply the discount when ready. This keeps audit clean and avoids surprise mass updates.

Each chain card has 4 actions

1. Edit Chain

Opens a form to change name, code, hasDiscount flag, chainDiscountPct, contract status (signed/pending/expired), effective/expiry dates, notes, and (since 2026-05-27) an optional chain-wide Ergos markup (baseCommission, range 1–100). When set, this value is inherited by every linked hotel that doesn't have its own per-hotel baseCommission override — see the Layer-2 cascade in Section 1. A small help line under the input notes that values ≥5% are usually paired with a negotiated chain discount; this is informational only and the form will save any value within range.

2. Manage Hotels — the LinkedHotelsDialog

The most useful action for chains with many hotels. Opens a side-drawer with two stacked sections:

  • Top section: hotels currently linked, with per-row Unlink button.
  • Bottom section: server-side hotel search (debounced 300ms) + country filter. Multi-select hotels to bulk-link.
  • Already-linked hotels are greyed out in the search results so you don't try to link them again.
  • Every link/unlink writes a per-hotel audit entry — the chain field, old/new value, and your reason text.

3. Apply discount to linked hotels

Only visible when the chain has a discount AND there are linked hotels. This is the chain → hotel cascade, made explicit. Clicking it:

  1. Asks for confirmation: "Apply 15% off to N hotel(s)?"
  2. Lets you add a reason text
  3. On confirm, writes each linked hotel's chainDiscountPct to the chain's value
  4. Creates one audit entry per affected hotel (not one bulk entry — per-hotel granularity)
Important: "Apply" is explicit, not automatic. If you change the chain's chainDiscountPct, linked hotels are NOT auto-updated. You have to click Apply. This is deliberate — it prevents silent mass changes and keeps each hotel's audit history intact.

4. Delete Chain

Only allowed if 0 hotels are linked. Otherwise blocked with a clear message: "Cannot delete — N hotel(s) still linked. Unlink them first."

4. Save Flow SaveConfirmModal

Legacy guardrails removed (2026-05-27). Earlier versions of the engine enforced three runtime guardrails (minErgosNetPct = 5%, maxAgentPctOfGross = 32%, minErgosPctOfGross = 50%). These were inherited from an early business spec and did not reflect the stakeholder-ratified commission picture (GDS → chain discount → Ergos markup → optional agency rebate → travel-agency markup). They were removed from the engine, frontend mirror, SaveConfirmModal, and right-rail UI. The only floor enforced today is the schema-level baseCommission ≥ 1.

Active lock-in: the no-guardrail state is asserted by 4 cypress tests in backoffice-app/cypress/e2e/commission-policy-save-flow.cy.ts (added in commit c99a218). Any accidental re-introduction of the Guardrails section in the right rail, the reason textarea, the "Cannot proceed" badge, or any of the 8 removed locale strings will fail the suite — so the doc and the code can't drift on this without breaking CI.

The SaveConfirmModal is a confirmation step — diff in, audit out. No runtime guardrails are evaluated.

When you click "Review and Save"

The modal shows you:

  1. Proposed changes — a table of every field that will change, scoped by entity (hotel, agency, agent, etc.).
  2. Cancel / Confirm Save — the only two actions. There is no warning state, no required reason, no hard block. Schema-level invariants (e.g. baseCommission ≥ 1) are still enforced server-side; if a save would violate them, the backend returns 400 and the dialog surfaces the message inline.

Centralized audit

On confirm, the page POSTs each layer's changes to a single centralized endpoint (PATCH /admin/commission-policy/layer/{scope}/{scopeId}) which writes the change AND an audit entry atomically. Every edit is therefore traceable to who changed what, when, and to what new value — independent of whether any threshold was crossed.

5. Booking-time Snapshot Compliance + dispute resolution

Every booking — single-room or multi-room — stores a frozen copy of the resolved commission policy at the moment of booking. This guarantees that if the policy changes later, the booking's original terms are still recoverable.

What's stored on each booking

// On every booking document (booking.model.ts): commissionSnapshot: { baseCommission: 8, // whole percent (8 = 8%) — Ergos markup at booking time chainDiscountPct: 15, // whole percent (15 = 15% off GDS net) rebatePct: 4, // whole percent (4 = 4% rebate) earningPct: 26, // whole percent (26 = 26% of gross) opsExpense: 6, // whole percent (6 = 6% of gross) resolvedAt: ISODate('2026-03-15T14:22Z'), } // Stored on the SAME booking doc but OUTSIDE commissionSnapshot — // these capture the agency-side markup. Same whole-percent integer convention: agencyCustomerPrice: 220.92, // dollars — what the agency charged the end customer agencyMarkupPctSnapshot: 15, // whole percent (15 = 15%) — agency's markup % at booking time
One consistent convention across the platform. All percentage fields — the 5 inside commissionSnapshot (driven by the commission engine) and the agency-side agencyMarkupPctSnapshot stored alongside it — are whole-percent integers. 15 means 15%, not 0.15. The agency markup lives outside the commission engine's math (it doesn't affect any payout calculation), but the storage format, validation rules, API contract, and input controls all follow the same integer pattern as every other percent field. The freeze logic in booking.service.ts writes the two snapshot blocks independently — one can succeed while the other fails (e.g. an agency without a configured defaultMarkupPct) — but their formats now match.

Where it's visible

On the backoffice booking detail page (/reservations/:id), a "Commission Snapshot" card appears below the price distribution table. It shows the 5 percentages, a "Frozen at booking time" badge, and the resolution timestamp.

On the agency app (agency-app) /bookings/:id page, a "Your Earnings (P&L)" card shows the agency's view: Ergos cost, customer paid, markup income (customerPrice − ergosSell), rebate income (ergosSell × rebatePct/100), and total profit — all computed from the same snapshot. There is also an aggregate Agency P&L page at /pnl (route GENERAL_ROUTES.pnl) listing per-booking earnings over a date range.

The key insight: if you change the global policy after a booking, the booking's snapshot stays unchanged. The Commission Snapshot card always shows what was agreed at that booking's quote time, regardless of how policy has evolved since.

6. Money Flow — Who Earns What All 4 parties

Sections 1-5 explained how Ergos prices a booking and protects its margins. But every booking involves 4 parties — the hotel, Ergos, the travel agency, and the sales agent — and each one makes money differently. This section makes the full money flow explicit, including the recently-shipped agency markup feature that lets the platform capture and report the agency's customer-facing price.

Two different "layer" concepts live in this system — don't confuse them:
  • 5-layer commission policy (Section 1) — how Ergos's INTERNAL policy is structured: Global → Hotel → Agency → Tier → Agent-Hotel Override
  • 4-layer pricing chain (this section) — how money flows from end customer back through to the hotel: GDS Net → Adjusted Cost → Ergos Sell → Agency Sell
They're complementary, not conflicting. The 5-layer policy DRIVES the pricing chain.

The agency markup — out of scope for Ergos's commission policy

Important boundary: Whatever markup a travel agency charges its final customer is the agency's own business decision. Ergos does not set it, does not enforce it, and does not factor it into any commission calculation or profitability check. The agency manages it from their own back office.

Layer 3 (Agency → Customer) sits outside our commission policy. The agency configures a defaultMarkupPct on their Profile page if they want to use the platform's optional invoicing helpers, but the value has zero effect on:

  • The Ergos sell price (what the agency pays Ergos)
  • The booking agent's commission
  • The Ergos net margin
  • The savability of a commission policy

The agency markup is captured purely as a convenience feature so the agency can run their own P&L view inside the platform if they choose. They can also leave it blank and run their pricing entirely in their own POS — Ergos doesn't care.

What Ergos actually controls and prices for

Because the agency markup is out of scope, Ergos's commission policy focuses exclusively on the two cost levers we do control:

LeverWhat it pays forWhy it matters
Booking agent commission (Layer 1, baseline 18–24% of Ergos gross) The agent who closes a booking with a customer for the agency This is the original commission engine — keeps existing booking-side agents paid fairly across hotel/chain/agency overrides
Sales-agent recruiting commission (new — see §11) The independent sales rep who recruits new travel agencies onto Ergos and supports them ongoingly This is our primary growth lever. Each productive agency a sales agent recruits compounds Ergos's volume against a near-fixed cost base. Detailed model in §11.

Everything else (hotel net rate, ops cost per booking, agency rebate) is either contractually negotiated upstream, infrastructurally driven, or fixed by partnership terms. The commission policy's job is to balance these two controllable costs against the Ergos net floor — nothing more.

7. Use Cases 10 worked examples

These are concrete scenarios you'll encounter, with the exact steps to execute them.

UC 1 · Chain onboarding

First-time setup of a new hotel chain

Scenario: Ergos signs an exclusive contract with Meliá for 15% off all 12 of their hotels in the catalog. The Meliá deal is signed and effective today.
  1. Go to /commission-chains → click + Add Chain
  2. Fill the form: name Meliá, code MELIA, hasDiscount ON, chainDiscountPct 15 (15% off), contractStatus signed, effectiveDate today, notes "Q3 master contract"
  3. Save → new card appears with "0 hotel(s)"
  4. Click Manage Hotels on the Meliá card
  5. In the search box type Meli → server returns matching hotels after 300ms
  6. Click all 12 Meliá hotels to select them → click Link 12 hotel(s)
  7. Back on the chain card → click ⚡ Apply discount to linked hotels
  8. Confirm dialog: "Apply 15% off to 12 hotel(s)?" → add reason "Initial chain contract activation" → confirm
Result: All 12 Meliá hotels now sell at 15% off cost basis. 12 audit entries are created (one per hotel), each tagged with your reason text. Future Meliá bookings will use chainDiscountPct 15 until renegotiated.
UC 2 · Chain renegotiation

Renegotiating a chain discount

Scenario: Iberostar agrees to a deeper discount (22% off, was 18%) for Q4. You need to push the new terms to all linked Iberostar hotels.
  1. Go to /commission-chains → click Edit Chain on Iberostar
  2. Change chainDiscountPct from 18 (18% off) to 22 (22% off)
  3. Update notes: "Q4 renewed contract — deeper discount"
  4. Save → chain card shows the new value
  5. Click ⚡ Apply discount to linked hotels
  6. Confirm: "Apply this chain's price adjustment (22) to all N linked hotel(s)?" → reason: "Q4 renegotiation activation" → confirm
Result: All linked Iberostar hotels now have chainDiscountPct 22. Each hotel gets an audit entry showing the change from 18 → 22. The chain itself also has audit entries for the chainDiscountPct edit.
UC 3 · Per-hotel exception

One-off commission adjustment for a single hotel

Scenario: Hotel Arts Barcelona is a luxury property and Ergos wants 10% markup there (default is 8%). Doesn't affect any other hotel.
  1. Go to /commission-policy → Hotels tab → search "Hotel Arts" → click on it
  2. Inheritance stack appears → on the Hotel layer card, change Base Commission from 8 to 10
  3. Yellow save bar appears at the bottom — click Review and Save
  4. SaveConfirmModal shows: 1 proposed change
  5. Click Confirm Save
Result: Only Hotel Arts now has 10% markup. The change is audited with scope=hotel. Other hotels are unaffected — this is a surgical override.
UC 4 · Bulk edit

Update commission across many hotels at once

Scenario: End of year — Ergos decides to raise all 5-star Hoteltec markups to 9% (from a mix of 7-8%). Doing this one hotel at a time would take hours.
  1. Go to /commission-policy → click Bulk Edit in the page header (top right)
  2. In the Bulk Edit drawer, set filter: Stars = 5, Vendor = Hoteltec
  3. Set values: Ergos markup = 9 (leave chainDiscountPct blank)
  4. Add reason: "End-of-year markup uplift for 5-star Hoteltec inventory"
  5. Click Preview → drawer shows "47 hotel(s) affected" with a sample table of old → new values per hotel
  6. If the preview looks right, type APPLY in the typed-confirm box
  7. Click Apply
Result: 47 hotels updated atomically. 47 audit entries are written, each with your reason. The drawer closes and the LeftRail refreshes so you can see the new values immediately.
UC 5 · Agent tier change

Promoting an agent to a higher tier

Scenario: M. Ruiz hit Q3 targets and is being promoted from Growth to Elite tier. Her earning percentage should bump from 18% to 24%.
  1. Go to /sales-agent → find M. Ruiz → click Edit
  2. Change Commission Tier dropdown: Growth → Elite
  3. Update Earning Percentage from 18 to 24 (within Elite's allowed range 21–26%)
  4. Save
Result: Future commission resolutions for M. Ruiz use Elite tier defaults. The change writes an audit entry with scope=agent, field=tier and field=earningPercentage. Bookings already made keep their snapshots — only new bookings use the new tier.
UC 6 · Special relationship

8. Formulas Reference The math

The commission engine is a pure function. Same inputs always produce the same outputs. These formulas are mirrored on both the backend (canonical, used at save and booking) and frontend (live preview in the right rail and Save modal).

The waterfall

Stored values are whole-percent integers (15 = 15%). The engine converts each percent input via pct(n) = n/100 before plugging into the formulas below. The expressions are written in decimal form for math clarity — substitute chainDiscountPct/100, baseCommission/100, etc. if you're working from stored values.

// Engine inputs are decimal-form (after pct() conversion): adjustedCost = gdsNet × (1 - chainDiscountPct) ergosSell = adjustedCost × (1 + baseCommission) rebateAmt = ergosSell × rebatePct agencyEffective = ergosSell − rebateAmt ergosGross = agencyEffective − adjustedCost agentEarning = ergosGross × earningPct opsAmt = ergosGross × opsExpense ergosNet = ergosGross − agentEarning − opsAmt

Margin KPIs (informational only — no runtime check)

// Derived from the waterfall outputs: ergosNetPct = ergosNet ÷ ergosSell agentPctOfGross = agentEarning ÷ ergosGross ergosPctOfGross = ergosNet ÷ ergosGross

These ratios are computed for display in the right rail and reporting surfaces. They are not enforced. The earlier guardrail constants (minErgosNetPct = 5, maxAgentPctOfGross = 32, minErgosPctOfGross = 50) were removed from the engine on 2026-05-27 because they were not part of the stakeholder-ratified commission picture.

Scenario A — Baseline (most common booking)

11. Sales-Agent Recruiting Model & Unit Economics Growth lever

This section defines a second commission flow alongside the booking-agent commission documented in §1–§9. Where the booking-agent commission pays the person who closes a sale to a customer, the sales-agent recruiting commission pays the person who recruits a travel agency onto the Ergos platform — and continues to share in that agency's lifetime booking volume.

Travel-agency recruitment is Ergos's primary growth channel. Many target agencies don't yet have a website or any digital ability to access international hotel inventory; they're currently constrained to whatever single GDS they have a manual relationship with. Onboarding them to Ergos gives them instant access to the full Dingus + Hotetec + Restel + Juniper aggregated inventory — and gives Ergos compounding booking volume against a near-fixed cost base.

What this section is NOT: It is not about the agency markup to the final customer. That's the agency's decision and is out of scope (see §6). This section is exclusively about what Ergos pays sales agents to recruit and retain travel agencies.

The two-flow commission architecture

Commission flowWho earns itWhat triggers itWhere it's documented
Booking-agent commission The agent who closes a booking with a final customer Per booking, % of Ergos gross, tiered (Starter → Strategic) §1–§9 (existing engine)
Sales-agent recruiting commission The independent rep who recruited the agency onto Ergos Per booking from that agency + signing bonus on onboarding This section (§11)

The two flows are additive on the same booking. A single booking from a recruited agency triggers both: the booking agent who handled the customer-facing transaction is paid via §1–§9; the sales agent who originally recruited the agency is paid additionally via §11.

Why this is a separate flow (not just an additional tier)

A sales agent's economics are fundamentally different from a booking agent's:

  • Booking agents earn per sale and have direct control over each transaction. Their commission ties to their closing performance.
  • Sales agents earn per booking from a portfolio of agencies they recruited. Their commission ties to partner activation and retention — they win by recruiting productive agencies and keeping them supported, not by closing individual bookings.

Mixing these into a single commission engine would muddy both incentive structures. They're modeled as parallel flows: booking-time agent commissions land in commissionSnapshot; sales-agent recruiting commissions are computed downstream against the productive bookings attributed to the recruited agency.

The cost base reality (mid-2026)

Sales-agent commissions are sized against Ergos's actual fixed-cost base, which at MVP scale is intentionally lean:

Cost categoryMonthly (CAD)Notes
Operations + payroll baseline ~$4,000 Current operating expenses (excluding infrastructure)
Alibaba Cloud infrastructure (k3s, PostgreSQL, Elasticsearch, Valkey) ~$1,000 Production-grade cluster, scales sublinearly with volume
Total fixed monthly ~$5,000 CAD (~$3,650 USD) Real burn rate — the number break-even is measured against
This is the controllable cost picture. Hotel net rates, agency rebates, and booking-agent commissions are either contractually fixed or already engine-managed. The two genuinely controllable cost levers Ergos has are (a) what we pay sales agents to recruit travel agencies, and (b) the infrastructure scaling curve. Everything else is downstream of those.

Sales-agent commission is computed per booking as a flat dollar amount ($2.00 USD recommended at MVP scale), not a percentage of gross. This keeps the math predictable for sales agents and easy to reason about across hotel categories with very different margins. The Ergos net floor (§4) still applies after the deduction.

Sales agent compensation — recommended model (conservative)

The recommended starting structure, after modeling against the cost base above:

ComponentAmountTrigger / Conditions
Signing bonus $500 USD one-time Paid after the recruited agency completes its first 10 paid bookings (filters out tire-kickers)
Per-booking recurring commission $2.00 USD per booking Paid on every booking made by an agency the sales agent recruited, for the lifetime of that agency's activity on Ergos
Productivity throttle drops to $1.00/booking If recruited agency drops below 50 bookings/month for 3 consecutive months — keeps sales agents motivated to support productivity, not sign-and-forget
Portfolio guardrail cap at 30% of monthly contribution Total recruiting commissions in any month cannot exceed 30% of Ergos's total contribution margin that month; excess rolls to next month. Protects against runaway costs in early scaling.

Alternative models considered and why they were rejected

ModelWhy rejected
Percentage of Ergos gross margin (instead of flat $2) Couples sales-agent income to variables they don't control (hotel net rate, booking-agent tier, ops cost). Recruiting reps need predictable per-booking economics to pitch productively.
Percentage of agency markup (sharing the agency's margin) Out of scope. The agency markup is the agency's business (see §6). Ergos doesn't share in it and won't allocate from it.
One-time recruiting fee only (no recurring) Creates sign-and-forget behavior. Sales agents have no incentive to keep recruited agencies productive once paid. Recurring commission ties sales-agent income to agency retention and health.
Aggressive rates ($3+/booking, $1,000 signing) At MVP volume (~1,000 bookings/month target), aggressive rates compress true net margin below the level needed to fund product investment. Reserved for targeted geographic pushes (e.g., a Spain or Mexico-specific recruitment sprint).

Travel agency's incentive to embark

For completeness — recruiting succeeds only if agencies have a compelling reason to join. The recruiting commission is a sales-side tool; the agency-side pitch rests on:

  1. Inventory access they don't have today. Many small/mid agencies have one GDS relationship. Ergos gives them aggregated access to Dingus + Hotetec + Restel + Juniper instantly — hundreds of thousands of properties internationally.
  2. No website required. They use the Ergos agency app to search, quote, and book. Their own digital presence is optional.
  3. 4% rebate on every Ergos sell, on top of whatever markup they charge their customer (which is their own business — they keep 100% of it).
  4. Chain discounts pushed through. When Ergos negotiates a chain rate (e.g., Meliá at 15%), the savings reduce the agency's net cost, expanding their markup headroom.
  5. Fast payouts via Tropipay, no credit-card processing infrastructure on their side, international payment support.
  6. A real human contact — the sales agent who recruited them stays as their ongoing support contact, with a financial incentive to keep them productive.

Open questions for next iteration

  • Should signing bonuses be tiered by recruited agency's projected volume (e.g., $500 default, $1,000 if agency commits to 100+ bookings/month minimum)?
  • Should sales agents be paid for agencies they recruit even after they leave the program (lifetime trailing commission), or should commissions cease when they exit?
  • Geographic recruitment sprints: should there be a 6-month doubled-rate window for agencies recruited in priority markets (Spain, Mexico, North America)?
  • Sales-agent tiers: parallel to booking-agent tiers (Starter → Strategic), should top recruiters unlock higher per-booking rates as a retention mechanism?
